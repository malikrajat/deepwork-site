The short version

Your DeepWork data is not collected. No account, no profile and nothing sold. Whatever you write in DeepWork is yours, and it stays on your own machine. Nothing is charged, and nothing is taken from you.

Five things exist outside it. The GoatCounter count of visits, the captcha on the contact form and a message you deliberately send me do not say who you are. The other two are Google: Analytics, which measures page views, and AdSense, which serves the advertising on the home page and the guides. Both use Google's own cookies and are described below. The website carries advertising; the DeepWork application does not.

Who is responsible

DeepWork is a personal open-source project, not a company. I am Rajat Malik, based in Gurgaon, India, and I am the person responsible for the processing described on this page. There is no data protection officer, no registered company and no business partner with access to anything described here.

Write to contact@rajatmalik.dev about this policy, or about anything that concerns your data.

This website

The site at deepwork.rajatmalik.dev is a set of static files. It has no server of its own, no database, no login and no comment form.

Hosting

The files are published through Cloudflare Pages, which processes the request the way any web host does: your IP address, the time, the file you asked for and your browser's description appear in its server logs. The source code, the downloads and the issue tracker are on GitHub, which handles those requests in the same ordinary way. I do not keep a visitor-level log from either. Cloudflare's handling is described in Cloudflare's privacy policy, and GitHub's in GitHub's privacy statement.

The visit count

The front page and the web app each send one pageview to GoatCounter (deepwork.goatcounter.com), so that "is anybody using this?" has an answer without DeepWork running a backend. The page sends the request itself — no third-party script is loaded to make it — and it contains:

  • the address of the page and its title;
  • the referring page, if your browser sends one, and any query string;
  • the width of your screen;
  • a flag saying whether the browser is automated, and a random value that stops the request being cached.

It writes no cookie, reads no identifier, and GoatCounter stores no IP address for it. What I can see is totals and grouped counts — pages, referrers, locations, browsers — never an individual visitor. A browser sending Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control is not counted, and neither is a local build, a preview deployment or the automated audit.

These documents are not counted. The privacy policy, the cookies page, the terms, the contact page and the guides send no GoatCounter pageview at all.

The Google tag

The front page also loads Google Tag Manager, which in turn loads Google Analytics. That is the one third-party script on the main page. Google receives the page address, the referring page, your IP address, a description of your browser and device, and an approximate location derived from the request, and it sets its own analytics cookies to recognise a returning browser.

Google processes that under its own privacy policy. The tag is loaded by the front page and the guides only — not by this policy, the terms, the cookies page or the contact page. A content blocker that stops Google is enough to opt out, and the page still works without it.

Advertising

The front page and the guides carry advertising served by Google AdSense. The DeepWork application carries none, and neither this policy nor the terms, cookies or contact pages load the advertising script.

Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on a user's prior visits to this website or other websites. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visits to this site and/or other sites on the internet. One of those is Google's DoubleClick cookie, which recognises a browser so an ad is not repeated and its performance can be measured. Google may receive your IP address, an approximate location derived from it, the page you are reading and device information, and it sets the cookies listed on the cookies page.

Third parties may place and read cookies on your browser, and may use web beacons, IP addresses and other identifiers to collect information as a result of ad serving on this site. Google's own explanation of what it does with that data is How Google uses data when you use our partners' sites or apps, and the consent rules this site follows are set out in Google's EU user consent policy.

Ads are served in labelled slots and are not formatted to look like navigation, a download button or this site's own content. Nobody is offered money, a reward or anything else in exchange for clicking an ad, and there is no request anywhere on the site to click an ad to support it.

You can opt out of personalised advertising at Google Ads Settings, or opt out of a third-party vendor's use of cookies for personalised advertising at aboutads.info. In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and Brazil, advertising and analytics storage start denied and Google's certified consent message asks for a choice before either is used; the cookies page explains the signals and how to change your mind.

Nothing is sold to an advertiser. There is no data broker and no audience list, and this site does not build a profile of you. Advertising pays for the domain and the time spent writing the guides; it has no influence on what they say.

The Site stats panel

The footer of the front page has a Site stats button. It reads the totals and the month's breakdowns back through GoatCounter's read-only API, with a token that can only read. Nothing is fetched until the panel is opened, and opening it reports nothing about your visit.

Cookies and third-party scripts

This site's own code sets no cookie and writes nothing to identify you beyond the theme preference described below. The typefaces and screenshots are shipped with the site rather than fetched from a font service or an image host. There is no social widget, no session recorder and no fingerprinting by the site itself.

Three third-party scripts are loaded by this site, and the first two are confined to the pages that need them. The front page and the guides load Google Tag Manager, which loads Google Analytics, and Google AdSense, which serves the advertising; both set Google's own cookies. The contact page loads hCaptcha (Intuition Machines, Inc.), which decides whether the form was filled in by a person and works by setting its own cookies and device signals. Each exists for a stated purpose — measurement and advertising on the reading pages, an unguarded public form on the contact page.

This policy, the terms, the cookies page and the contact page do not load the Google tags. With JavaScript turned off, the page still reads and the contact form falls back to email. hCaptcha's handling is described in hCaptcha's privacy policy, and every cookie the site can be served is listed on the cookies page.

What is stored on your device

One preference of this site's own is stored. It lives in localStorage, which is not a cookie: it is a file in your browser's own storage, it is never sent with a request, and clearing site data removes it.

Name What it holds Why
deepwork-site-theme dark or light So the theme you chose is the theme you get on the next page, without a flash of the other one. It is read on this site only and never transmitted.
Your DeepWork data Tasks, sessions, habits, journal entries and settings — only in the web app The app works. It is written by the app into your browser's storage or into a local database, and it never leaves the device. See below.

The Google Analytics and Google AdSense cookies described under This website are also stored in your browser when the front page's or a guide's tags run. They are Google's cookies, not this site's, and the documents do not set them; the cookies page lists the ones the site can be served. Apart from the theme preference and those cookies, this site stores nothing in your browser: no fingerprint, no identifier of its own and no preference beyond the theme you chose. The captcha on the contact page keeps its own cookies, which are hCaptcha's and are described above, not this site's.

The web app

The browser build at app.deepwork.rajatmalik.dev has no account and no sign-in. Everything you enter is kept in your own browser storage, on your own device, and is not sent anywhere. The web app can also be installed, and then it keeps working offline.

It counts visits with the same counter and the same promises — one pageview per load, no cookie, no stored IP address, and the page never sending a task or a journal entry. Settings → Count my visits turns that off; with the switch off, the counter is never loaded. The desktop build is never counted, and there is no collection of your tasks, your journal or anything else you write — not in aggregate, not anonymously, not at all.

Clearing your browser's site data deletes that data as well. To move it or to keep it, use the app's own JSON backup.

The desktop app

The Windows, macOS and Linux builds keep everything in a local SQLite database on your computer. There is no DeepWork server, no account and no sync: nothing you record is uploaded, and there is no telemetry about your work.

The desktop app makes three kinds of network request, and no others:

  • a release check — it asks the public GitHub API whether a newer version exists. GitHub sees the request and your IP address; the app sends no identifier of its own;
  • a download you press — an installer or update comes from GitHub's release page;
  • a link you open — the app opens it in your browser, where the destination's own policy applies.

The app's logs are local files on your machine, written so that a fault can be understood without anyone connecting to your computer.

Messages you send

You can write to contact@rajatmalik.dev for anything, or to support@rajatmalik.dev for help installing or using the app. A message sent by email passes through your mail provider and mine, and arrives in my mailbox.

I keep it for as long as it takes to answer, and afterwards for as long as ordinary correspondence is kept. It is not added to a mailing list, not sold, not shared, not used for advertising, and not used to train a model. Ask me to delete a thread and I will delete it.

The contact page has a form. Pressing Send posts the fields you filled in — your name, your email address, the topic and the message — to Web3Forms, which forwards them to contact@rajatmalik.dev as an email. The browser also sends its usual request headers, including your IP address, to that service. Nothing is stored on this site: it is a set of static files with no server and no database. With JavaScript turned off, the button composes the message in your own email app instead, so it travels through your provider and mine.

A form submission is kept no longer than the email it becomes. Web3Forms processes it only to deliver it and keeps its own logs under its privacy policy. Once it arrives, the message is treated exactly like one you send by email, as described above. The captcha on the form is hCaptcha, described under This website.

There is no phone number for DeepWork and none is requested. Nothing on this site asks for a date of birth, an address, a payment detail or an identity document.

Your rights

Because there is no account and no profile, there is very little to hold. I hold only what you choose to send me; Google holds the analytics data described above under its own policy. Where laws such as the GDPR or India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 apply, you may ask to:

  • be told what is held about you, and receive a copy;
  • have it corrected or erased;
  • object to being contacted, or withdraw a consent you gave;
  • complain to your local data protection authority.

Write to contact@rajatmalik.dev and I will act on the request. There is no automated decision-making about you anywhere in this project.

Children

This site and the app are not directed at children. There is no account and no profile, the advertising is not directed at children, and the Google tags described above are not configured to target them. The site is not tagged as child-directed in Google's systems, because it is not directed to children under 13. If a child writes to me, the message is treated like any other correspondence and deleted on request.
