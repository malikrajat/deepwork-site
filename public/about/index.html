About

About DeepWork and this site

A free, local-first focus timer made by one person — and a website that tries to describe it accurately rather than flatter it. This page says who makes both, how the claims are checked, and how the writing is paid for.

Who writes this

DeepWork, this website and the guides are made by Rajat Malik, a frontend architect and engineering lead based in Gurgaon, India. He is a Senior Lead Software Engineer at Siemens, where he has worked since 2020, and was at Siemens Energy and HCL before that. He studied Computer Science at the Ajmer Institute of Technology.

The app is a personal project, not a company product. There is no team behind it, no support desk and no sales call. That is also why the site is written the way it is: there is nobody to hand a marketing claim to, and no reason to make one.

Outside the day job he publishes MIT-licensed npm libraries, writes The Angular Insider newsletter, and speaks about web performance and frontend architecture. None of that is required to use DeepWork; it is here because a visitor is entitled to know who is talking.

What DeepWork is

DeepWork is a focus timer, task manager and habit tracker for Windows, macOS, Linux and any modern browser. It keeps its data in a local database on your own machine: no account, no cloud sync and no telemetry on your work. It is MIT-licensed, free, and there is no paid tier.

It is also a single-person tool. There are no shared boards, no collaboration features and no native iOS or Android app. The common questions say so directly, and the comparison table is written to be checked rather than believed.

The application is a separate repository from this website: malikrajat/deepwork. Reading it is the most reliable way to verify anything on this site.

How the claims are checked

Every number this site states about the app — timer defaults, water ranges, reminder ladders, export columns, breakpoints — was read out of the application's source. If a number here disagrees with the app, the app is right and this site is wrong.

Every screenshot is of a production build of the real application, captured from a working state. There are no mock-ups, no retouched screens and no stock photographs. The social preview card is built from the site's own chrome.

The site publishes no user counts, download figures, star counts, ratings, testimonials or revenue numbers, because the project does not publish any. There are no invented reviews on this site and no rating in its structured data. A claim that survives being checked is worth more than a stronger one that does not.

The guides are original to this site: no article is copied, syndicated or rewritten from another publication, and no page is a reprint of somebody else's work. Every page is written and reviewed before it is published, which is also why there is no automatically generated article feed here.

How the writing is paid for

The app is free and open source, and this site takes no payment from anyone. The domain and the time spent writing the guides are paid for by advertising on the home page and the guide pages: Google AdSense, loaded through Google's own consent platform. The cookies page describes exactly what it loads, and the privacy policy says what Google receives.

There are no sponsored articles, no affiliate links and no paid placements. Advertising has no influence on what the guides say — one of them argues that you may not need an app at all, which is not a sentence written to sell a subscription.

The ads follow Google's publisher policies, and the site is built to keep them visible as ads rather than as part of the page. They are labelled by Google, never styled to look like navigation, a download button or this site's own content, and never placed on the legal documents, the contact page, an error page or inside the application. Nobody is offered money or a reward for clicking one, nobody is asked to click one to support the site, and I do not click the ads on my own site.

If an ad appears that is unsuitable for this site, tell me at contact@rajatmalik.dev with the page and, if you can, the advertiser — it can be blocked. Google's own AdSense Program policies and Publisher Policies are the rules the site is checked against.

The DeepWork application itself carries no advertising. Advertising is on the home page and the guides on this website, and nowhere else.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, out of date or clearer than it should be, write to contact@rajatmalik.dev. That includes a claim about the app, a broken link, a screenshot that no longer matches, or a sentence that describes something the app cannot do. A correction changes the page and the date printed on it; the privacy policy, terms and guides all carry one.

Bugs in the application itself go to the issue tracker so they can be fixed in the code rather than described around.

The documents

The legal and practical pages are separate documents, each readable without JavaScript:

  • Privacy policy — what is and is not collected.
  • Cookies and choices — every cookie and how to change a choice.
  • Terms of use — the licence, the warranty and the law.
  • Contact — one form, two addresses and the issue tracker.
