Quiet & attention

A pause that pauses everything

Pause, mute and focus used to live only in the system tray — which meant no way in on a Linux desktop without tray icons, in a browser tab, or on a phone. They are now one panel in four places, and each control means exactly one thing.

The same panel opens from the sidebar’s footer, the phone’s top bar, Settings and Ctrl+Shift+Q. The tray menu is one more caller, so Windows, macOS and Linux cannot disagree about what “Pause for 30 minutes” does.

The panel, shown on its own. Its three controls are independent: focus leaves the reminders running, a pause holds them, and a mute only stops the sound.

Answerable alerts

The clock can be driven from the notification

A finished session posts four answers with the alert: Pause, Next, Resume and Hold up. Next moves on and starts the next session, so answering is all it takes to carry on. The in-app card draws the same four, which keeps the alert answerable on platforms whose notifications cannot report a press.
